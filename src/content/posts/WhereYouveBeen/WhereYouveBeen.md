Jamie had always been told he’d find company when he’d least expected it. When he settled down and finally had his life in order, someone would come along to disrupt the status quo.

After moving out to his small cabin in the woods, he found his days empty. Having only himself to support, a few odd jobs for his distant neighbors was more than enough to cover his expenses. So he took up a new hobby, exploring the land around his home. It started small, a walk down a new trail, a wrap around a nearby pond, but in time this also became routine. 

That was, until he found the cave.

On one of his walks through his usual route, a low howl came from beyond the tree line. He had never heard anything like it. He turned and followed the sound. An icy breeze tickled his ankles as he rounded the bend. Before him, nestled far beyond the path on the side of a cliff face, lay the gaping mouth of the cave.

Something compelled him closer. The tantalizing mystery drawing him in like a fish to a lure. The entrance was cold and damp. The muddy floors slicked beneath him with every step. Far beyond where the sun’s light could reach, Jamie could feel a breeze pushing frigid air out from within. He had to know more.

The cave enveloped his thoughts. Every morning Jamie would pack for his latest expedition, bringing enough supplies to last multiple days. He’d stuff a notebook filled with copious maps he’d made in his satchel. Each day he’d probe a little deeper. The caverns seemed endless, each path fracturing out into countless dead-ends and turnarounds. Today was going to be his biggest day yet. He had just found a new branch during his last expedition and was dying to get back out there and follow it.

---

Jamie set the lantern on the cavern floor and took a deep breath, reaching into his satchel and pulling free a parchment and charcoal. By his recount, he should be the deepest he’d ever been. He scribbled the last few tunnels on the parchment in the dancing light of his lantern.

“Hi stranger!”

Jamie spun around and shrieked. A figure about half his size stood before him. Their spindly arms stretched to the floor with a long-pointed nose between glowing yellow eyes. Jamie scuttled away from the dark figure, dropping his notebook.

“What is this?” The figure said, picking up notebook and flipping through it.

“G-give that back!”

“Ooo, what nice handwriting!” They ran their hands across the drawings.

“Stop! You’ll smudge it!”

The figure stopped and eyed the terrified boy. They closed the notebook and walked over. Jamie tried to shuffle away; his back pressed again the cave walls. The figure lent a hand. “Nice to meet you!” Jamie’s face scrunched in confusion. He took the figure’s hand and was hoisted to his feet.

“Who are you?” Jamie said.

“I’m a shade. I live here.” they said, placing a hand on their chest.

“I see…” Jamie said. He found himself forming the start of several sentences with his mouth, but no sound came out. He was flabbergasted to have found something else down here.

“Everything alright?” the shade said, tilting their head to the side.

“Oh- nothing. Umm… Is anyone else down here, by chance?” he said, looking around the empty cavern.

“No. Just me. I’ve seen a couple spiders pass through, but they don’t talk much.”

“Huh.” Jamie said. Although the figure’s appearance still shook him, their cheerful conversation felt disarming, “I’m Jamie.” He said, holding out a carful arm.

“Nice to meet you!” The shade stretched their gaunt arm out and clasped his hand, shaking it aggressively. “Oh this is just lovely, I’m so glad someone’s here. You seem lost. Would you like the grand tour? There’s so much I’d love to share.”

Jamie blinked. For being all alone, the shade had a lot to say. “Oh yeah? Like what?”

The shade’s eyes lit up “C’mon, I’ll show you!” They led him further into the caves. “Oh, this is so exciting! I’ve been dreaming of this. Do you like reading? I love reading. Oh! There’s also the grand hall and the treasury, how lovely!” The shade look Jamie deep into the cavern to show him all their favorite spots.

Turns out, deep below the upper caverns lay a sprawling underground palace. The shade toured Jamie around, showing him all the highlights. The two passed through massive dining halls, sweeping treasuries, and extensive libraries. All the while, the two chatted with each other.

Jamie learned lots about the shade. How they loved to read and write, draw pictures, collect mushrooms and fungi, and noodle on their instrument. The longer the two talked the closer Jamie felt to them. Such a lively and intelligent creature in such a lonely place. He felt an instant connection.

“And this is my bedroom!” The shade said. The two were nearing the end of the tour.

“Looks cozy.” Jamie said. The small room contained a personal bookcase lined with novels, a reading chair, and a small bedframe covered in moss. “You don’t use this often?” he said, pointing at the bedframe.

“Oh I do! Moss makes for the best bedding. Give it a try sometime.”

“Ah.” Jamie said, whipping his eye. Speaking of bedding, he had lost track of time. How long had he been down here? He felt a sudden wash of exhaustion overtake him. “It must be getting late; I should get back to my home.”

“You’ll come back though, yeah?” the shade asked.

“You kidding? Of course! You gotta show me some of those novels you were talking about earlier.”

The shade’s eyes lit up. “Oh, that’s just great, I look forward to it!”

Jamie let out a smile. In the most unlikely of places, he had made a friend. He’d continue to visit the shade, often going days without returning to the surface. The two would spend hours devouring the books in the palace library, tending to the shade’s personal mushroom garden, or wander through the extensive passageways of the caverns above. He developed a kindship with them, like they were able to see past his act and view the real person he was. Maybe he didn’t need anyone up top, he’d think. Everything he’d want in a person was already down here.

Something nagged at him, however. A lingering thought at the back of his head. If the shade wanted company so bad, why stay down here alone? And what even was this place? During one of his visits, he decided to ask.

The two stopped before a large pool of water. Towards the back cavern wall, lay a gigantic white crystal. The sounds of the waterfall echoed off the slick stone walls.

“Hey Shade, mind if I ask you something?”

“Go ahead.” They said, kneeling down by the edge of the water.

“How’d all this stuff get here?” 

“Dunno. It’s always been here.”

“And you seriously have no idea how you got down here either?

The shade shrugged. “I wasn’t, then I was. Isn’t that how it was for you?”

 “I suppose…. Why stay down here if you’re so lonely?”

“Can’t leave.” The shade said, running their hand across the surface of the pool.

“Why not?”

“It hurts. Anytime I go too far away I feel like I’m burning.”

“Well, that’s unfortunate.” Jamie said, kneeling beside the shade.

“But hey,” The shade said, turning to Jamie “I met you, and you’re cool. That’s something!”

Jamie smiled. “I suppose it is.”

The shade skimmed their hand through the pool. Jamie noticed an ashy color of the back of their hand.

“What’s going on there?” He asked.

“Oh this?” they said, holding up their arm. The backs of their hands were flaky and withered. “I dunno. I think I’m dry.”

“That can happen to shades?”

“I guess so, but this helps.” The shade motioned to the pool. “Speaking of…” They pulled their arm out of the water and took a few steps back. “Last one in smells like sulfur!” They sprinted to the water’s edge.

“Hey!” Jamie said. The shade vaulted off the stone floor and tucked their legs in. They landed in the water with a large splash, drenching Jamie.

“You didn’t give me enough time!” Jamie said, wiping the water from his eyes.

The shade’s head poked out from the surface of the water, a big toothy smile on their face. “Sorry man, you gotta be quicker than that.”

He’d continue his visits to the shade, although something felt different. Each time he’d come down, he’d notice the shade’s condition would worsen. It started with their hands, but the ash had spread across their body. The shade always reassured him that they were fine, always finding a new excuse for their condition. “I just slept weird last night, I’ll be ok.” “I’m just thirsty, want to stop by the pool?” The reasons changed but they shade kept getting worse.

Jamie squinted at the bookshelf and rubbed his chin. A coarse sensation tickled his fingers. Hair? It had been a couple months since he met the shade, and he was knee deep in another multi-day trip. Today he was alone however, the shade said that they had some important matters to attend to deep within the caverns, and let Jamie peruse the library. That was hours ago, and his head was spinning.

The library was in complete organizational disarray. Every one of the towering bookcases seemed like their contents were placed randomly. Jamie panned across the next shelf. 107 Ways to Prepare Lindholm Seeds, Curses and Hexes: A Guide for Simpletons, Duke Filigree XXI: A Complete Autobiography. Part 8 of 37. This was getting nowhere fast. He sighed and furrowed his brow.

There was suspicion of the shade’s condition growing within him. It gnawed at him. There had to be something going on. He was hoping answers might be hidden away between these thousands of pages, but no luck so far. Not a book or note about shades in sight.

“Shade would know.” He said to himself.

“And I would!”

Jamie jumped and looked up. The shade’s head was poking from the banister with a huge toothy smile.

“Good lord- You gotta stop doing that.” Jamie said, catching his breath.

“Oh, it’s just a little fun, don’t be such a wart.”

He gave the shade an annoyed glance. “You’re lucky you’re cool.” The shade chuckled. Jamie eyed the shade. Their once vibrant eyes looked clouded and sunken. Their normally soot black body appeared ashy and faded.

“You doing alright up there?” He asked.

“Oh I’m alright, don’t you worry about it.”

Jamie peered at them. “I thought you said you’d be busy today.”

“I was. Now I’m not.” The shade said, holding a clump of moss with blue speckles over the banister. “This is the biggest one I’ve found yet! I’ll be sleeping soundly for weeks. It’s been rough I’ll tell you what.”

“Ah, right…” Jamie said, feigning interest. His attention was held on concern for them.

“What were you looking for?” the shade asked.

“I was looking for a book about The Greater Plateau, but I can’t find one in this mess.” Jamie said, waving his hand in front of the bookcases. In truth, the two were miles underneath the plateau, but Jamie hoped that saying this instead might hide his intentions.

“Bookcase 82 shelf 3, it’s the big red book next to the one about Corin’s fall.” the shade said, pointing to the bookshelf behind Jamie.

“You read it already?”

The shade shook their head. “Nah, encyclopedias are boring.”

“True.” he said, pulling the leatherbound novel off the shelf and eyeing the cover. “I’m gonna go read this in my chair.”

Jamie made his way over to the reading nook and took a seat in the big red chair. He opened the book and flipped to the table of contents, skimming until he found an entry labeled “Shades”.

# Shades
The shade; often confused with specters, shadows, and wraiths; is an undead creature and a member of the genus Inmortui. They are often found inhabiting places of ruin: be it an abandoned castle or forgotten manor. Indeed, the larger the influence the structure had in life, the larger the likelihood a shade will take up residence once it’s deserted.

Depending on the size of the ruin, the appearance a resident shade changes. Smaller huts and cabins may have a shade that take incorporeal forms. A black mist or see-through shadow-esc figure. Larger structures may have a shade that takes on a corporeal body, often with short statures and exaggerated human features.

Jamie looked up from the book. He saw the shade intensely scribbling a blue stone on a blank piece of paper across the library. They surely fit the bill for where they were. He kept reading.

Should, however, a ruin be demolished or rehabilitated, the shade’s presence will diminish accordingly. Because of this, shades are solitary creatures, believed to be physical manifestations of the longing left behind by the place they inhabit. Depending on the structure, shades may evaporate as soon as someone enters if their residence is small enough, making studying these creatures exceedingly difficult.

Jamie’s heart sunk. He skimmed ahead, looking for any exceptions or uncertainty in the text.

“That book got what you’re looking for?” The shade said, spooking Jamie yet again. He clapped the book shut.

“Dunno yet.” He lied, tucking the book off to his side. “Whatcha drawin’ over there?”

The shade held up a piece of paper. Scribbled on it was a picture of a starry night “Just working on a new piece, found some blue rocks while I was out and about and I think they make just the loveliest highlights.

A foul odor wafted from the page. “Whatever it is, it sure stinks.” Jamie said, plugging his nose.

“I know, right? I love it.”

Jamie watched the shade admire their drawing. What he read had to be true, but he still held out hope he missed something. He thumbed the spine of the book. “Hey, mind if I borrow this? I’d like to keep reading it later.”

“Sure! Be my guest.”

Jamie let out a sigh and tucked the book in his satchel. He didn’t want the shade to know what was inside.

“I think I’m gonna head up for now. Its been a minute.” Jamie said, slinging the satchel over his shoulder.

“Aww, leaving so soon?” The shade said, pretending to pout.

“Oh don’t give me that, I’ll be back.”

“Well, hang on!” the shade said, running over to one of the bookshelves and pulling free a novel. ”Would you mind getting me something?”

“If it’s another ask for satyrs milk, the answer is still no.” Jamie said, kneeling to the shade’s level.

“Oh no no no, it’s something else.” They started, flipping through the book. “There’s this tree that sprouts these beautiful flowers that I’ve always wanted to see. Would you be able to bring me some?” The shade turned the book around to reveal a picture of a massive white tree with purple flowers sprouting from its branches. 

“I can see what I can do. I think they grow not too far from here. When spring comes I can bring ‘em down here for you.”

“Oh thank you so much!” The shade said, hugging Jamie. He patted their back.

“It’s no problem. Alrighty, I better head out, See you next time.”

Jamie returned to the surface and poured over the encyclopedia he borrowed, reading the entry from front to back and checking every footnote. Everything came back to the same truth: Shade’s could only be alone. By him visiting them as much as he did, he’d eventually cause them to disappear.

He didn’t sleep that night, tossing and turning over what to do. On the one hand, he could keep it to himself. A small part of him hoped the book wasn’t true, but too many other descriptions already overlapped. His mind was wracked with indecision, waffling between options, none of which seemed appealing.

Weeks passed. Weeks then turned into months. Jamie stopped going on his walks. Every time he tried, he’d find himself only able to think about the shade beneath his feet. He’d approach the cave entrance, thinking today would be the day he’d break the news, but he’d always walk away. After a while he’d stop trying, staying indoors, and isolating himself from the world. He’d find cold moments of bliss where he didn’t have to remember the shade. Summer turned to fall, turned to winter, then to spring.

Jamie peered out the blinds. The trees were in full bloom outside his cabin. He rubbed his eyes, time had become a blur, each day the same as the last. He eyes the forest outside his cabin. The trees had begun to bud, thousands of tiny green dots littered their branches. Off in the distance, he saw a small purple dot mixed in the tree line. He squinted to get a better look. A tall white tree on the side of a mountain face had begun sprouting purple flowers. A hot wash of shame flooded over him. He remembered his promise, the last wish the shade gave him before he left. He pulled himself out of his bed, slipped on his shoes, and grabbed a pair of shears.

Jamie took a deep breath and fully exhaled, shimmying himself in between the rocks. It was a long way down, and he’d have to compress his body to fit through the narrow slot. He immerged from the other end of the chute, loose gravel following behind. He picked himself up and dusted the dirt off his clothes. He reached back into the chute felt around for his satchel. The only indication of the room around him being deep echoes of water droplets falling from the ceiling. With any luck, he hadn’t been noticed. Jamie fished around his satchel until he felt his lamp. He pulled it free and twisted the lighter.

The lantern sparked to life. Bathing the cave in a dim orange light. Jamie stood up and held the lamp aloft. Thousands of stalagmites littered the floor of the massive cavern, with more stalactites above. He panned the lantern around the room, looking for any signs of life. Luckily, he was alone. Quietly slinging his satchel over his shoulder, he pressed on into the carven, trying to remember the path.

“Where’ve you been?”

Jamie whirled around. A small black figure stood atop a large stalagmite, yellow eyes peering at him from the void.

“Hello.” he said, wincing. He was hoping he could make a clean drop.

“It’s been pretty lonely down here” The figure shuffled down their perch. “No one to talk to…”

“Well… that really wasn’t my choice.”

“Was it?” The shade snapped. “I waited; you know. Every day I thought of you. Where you went. What you might be doing. Why you left.” The shade looked at the ground, shuffling the gravel with their feet.

“Well… I’m sorry.” Jamie said.

“Do you have any idea what it’s like?! To feel loved and left behind?” The shade shot their eyes up at Jamie, tear streaking down their face. “You just left without saying a word after you took that stupid book!”

“Listen, I-“

“SHUT UP!” The shade screamed, breaking into a full sob. “I only ever had you…  you left without a word. It’s like you left me to die down here… it’s like I never knew you.”

Jamie reached for the shade but retracted his arm. As much as he wanted to comfort them, he knew things couldn’t go back. He knew too much, and falling back in with them again wasn’t an option. He stood silently, letting the shade dob on the floor until they calmed down.

“Why’d you come back?” the shade said, taking deep breaths as they wiped their face. Jamie reached into his satchel and pulled out the book. The shades eyes turned from sorrow to anger.

“Keep the damn book. You just came back for you.” The shade said, slapping the book to his chest and walking back into the darkness.

“Wait!” Jamie said, reaching out to the void. His words bounced off the slick cave walls, then everything fell silent. He was alone once more.

He stuck a hand back into his satchel and pulled out the purple flower. He rotated between his fingers, watching the petals dance under the light of his lantern. He placed it on the muddy floor of the cavern, picked up his lantern, and made his way towards the exit.
